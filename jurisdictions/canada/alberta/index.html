Executive Summary

Customs clearance in Alberta is the federal Canadian customs function through which goods entering or leaving Canada through Alberta airports, inland freight facilities, rail terminals, highway networks and related customs locations are reported, accounted for, assessed, examined where necessary and released or exported under Canadian customs law. It is not a separate Alberta provincial customs system. The controlling customs authority is the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA), while Alberta remains relevant through its air-cargo gateways, inland logistics, energy and industrial supply chains, North American market connections and tax environment.

Operationally, customs clearance commonly begins before goods reach an Alberta gateway. Businesses need to identify the goods, route, commercial parties, importer of record, customs broker or other accounting party, applicable Customs Tariff classification, value for duty, origin, admissibility requirements, relevant Other Government Department controls and supporting records required for Canadian import or export processing.

Alberta's customs significance is shaped by its inland, air-cargo, industrial and energy-focused trade environment. Calgary Air Commercial, CBSA Office 701, provides commercial release and export e-Longroom channels and is located at 175 Aero Way North East in Calgary. Alberta commercial operations also interact with CBSA trade operations coverage for Manitoba, Saskatchewan, Alberta and the Northwest Territories. Calgary International Airport, Edmonton International Airport, inland rail and truck corridors, customs warehouses and regional distribution infrastructure support the handling of international goods through the province.

Alberta has a distinctive provincial tax position in the Canadian import environment. Federal GST can apply to imported goods and is generally collected by CBSA at importation or when goods are taken out of bond for use, subject to applicable exemptions and importer circumstances. Alberta does not levy a general provincial sales tax and does not participate in the HST system. The absence of a general Alberta PST does not remove federal Customs, duty, GST, excise or other regulatory obligations. It means that, unlike Ontario, Quebec, British Columbia, Manitoba and Saskatchewan, Alberta has no general provincial consumption-tax layer on imported goods.

Purpose

The purpose of customs clearance through Alberta is to ensure that goods entering or leaving Canada via Alberta airports, inland facilities, rail terminals, road networks and related customs locations are processed through the federal Canadian customs framework in a controlled, documented and legally coherent manner.

It exists to support admissible and properly accounted goods, correct tariff, duty and federal GST treatment, lawful cargo release or export, effective border control and alignment between the importer or exporter, shipment, CBSA, carriers, customs brokers and relevant regulatory authorities. Alberta's no-general-PST position should be recognised as a provincial tax distinction, not as an exemption from federal customs and import obligations.
