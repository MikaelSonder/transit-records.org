Executive Summary

Customs clearance in Ontario is the federal Canadian customs function through which goods entering or leaving Canada through Ontario airports, marine ports, rail facilities, Canada-United States land borders and related customs locations are reported, accounted for, assessed, examined where necessary and released or exported under Canadian customs law. It is not a separate Ontario provincial customs system. The controlling customs authority is the Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA), while Ontario remains relevant through its gateway infrastructure, regional logistics, commercial market and provincial HST component on qualifying imported goods.

Operationally, customs clearance commonly begins before goods reach an Ontario gateway. Businesses need to identify the goods, route, commercial parties, importer of record, customs broker or other accounting party, applicable Customs Tariff classification, value for duty, origin, admissibility requirements, relevant Other Government Department controls and supporting records required for Canadian import or export processing.

Ontario's customs significance is shaped by its dense international transport and trading environment. Toronto Lester B. Pearson International Airport is a major air-cargo and CBSA commercial operations gateway; the CBSA Commercial Operations Hub/Central office is located in Mississauga. Ontario also has marine cargo functions in Toronto and Hamilton, extensive Canada-U.S. land-border crossings in the Windsor-Detroit, Niagara and Sarnia corridors, rail and intermodal infrastructure, bonded facilities and distribution networks serving Ontario, Québec, the Midwest and wider North American markets.

Ontario has an important provincial tax interface within Canada's federal import system. The Canada Border Services Agency assesses and collects duties and taxes on imported goods, including GST/HST. Goods imported for use or consumption in Ontario may be subject to the 13 percent Ontario HST, consisting of a 5 percent federal component and an 8 percent Ontario component, subject to applicable exemptions, importer status and accounting rules. This produces a two-layer operating context: federal Canadian customs clearance through CBSA and CARM, together with Ontario HST analysis on qualifying imported goods.

Purpose

The purpose of customs clearance through Ontario is to ensure that goods entering or leaving Canada via Ontario airports, ports, rail facilities, Canada-U.S. land borders and related customs locations are processed through the federal Canadian customs framework in a controlled, documented and legally coherent manner.

It exists to support admissible and properly accounted goods, correct tariff, duty and tax treatment, lawful cargo release or export, effective border control and alignment between the importer or exporter, shipment, CBSA, carriers, customs brokers and relevant regulatory authorities. Where goods are imported for use or consumption in Ontario, the process can also require Ontario HST consideration.

Primary Outcome

A goods movement lawfully reported, accounted for, released, exported or otherwise processed under federal Canadian customs requirements through an Ontario gateway, supported by correct commercial data, documentary evidence, tariff and value-for-duty treatment, origin and admissibility readiness and, where applicable, proper GST/HST treatment including the Ontario HST component.
