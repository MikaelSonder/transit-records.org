Executive Summary

Customs clearance in Georgia is the federal United States Customs function through which goods entering or leaving the country through Georgia seaports, airports and related Customs facilities are presented, entered, reviewed and released or exported under U.S. Customs law. It is not a separate Georgia state Customs system. The controlling Customs authority is U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP), while Georgia can remain relevant to sales and use tax, commercial operations, port logistics, manufacturing, agricultural trade and the destination use of imported goods.

Operationally, Customs clearance commonly begins before goods arrive at a Georgia gateway. Businesses need to identify the goods, route, commercial parties, importer of record, customs broker or other filing party, applicable Harmonized Tariff Schedule classification, Customs value, country of origin, admissibility requirements, relevant Partner Government Agency controls and supporting records required for U.S. entry or export processing.

Georgia's Customs significance is strongly connected to Savannah, CBP Port Code 1703, which serves as a key Atlantic commercial cargo and vessel-clearance location. The CBP port record identifies cargo processing, vessel entrance and clearance, operations, fines/penalties/forfeitures and a 24-hour after-hours contact channel. The Port of Savannah also participates in CBP's Advanced Qualified Unlading Approval Lane programme, commonly known as AQUA Lane, through which eligible vessels may begin unloading and loading cargo while final vessel clearance is under way, subject to programme and Customs conditions. Georgia's ports, airports, inland logistics facilities and distribution networks also support Southeast U.S. and international supply chains.

Georgia also has a separate state tax interface. The Georgia Department of Revenue states that use tax is imposed on non-exempt items brought into Georgia and on the first instance of use, consumption, distribution or storage in Georgia of tangible personal property purchased at retail outside Georgia. If property was used outside Georgia for six months or less before first use in Georgia, tax is imposed on the purchase price at the state and local sales-tax rate; property used outside Georgia for more than six months may be taxed using the lower of purchase price or fair market value. This creates a two-layer operating context: federal Customs clearance at the U.S. border and separate Georgia sales or use-tax analysis where goods are used, distributed, stored or consumed in Georgia.

Purpose

The purpose of Customs clearance through Georgia is to ensure that goods entering or leaving the United States via Georgia seaports, airports and associated Customs facilities are processed through the federal U.S. Customs framework in a controlled, documented and legally coherent manner.

It exists to support admissible and properly entered goods, correct tariff and duty treatment, lawful cargo release or export, effective Customs control and alignment between the importer or exporter, shipment, CBP, carriers, customs brokers and relevant regulatory authorities. Where goods are brought into Georgia for storage, use, distribution or consumption, the process can also require separate Georgia sales or use-tax consideration.

Primary Outcome

A goods movement lawfully entered, released, exported or otherwise processed under federal U.S. Customs requirements through a Georgia port, airport or related Customs facility, supported by correct entry data, documentary evidence, tariff and Customs-value treatment, admissibility readiness and, where applicable, separate Georgia state sales or use-tax compliance consideration.
