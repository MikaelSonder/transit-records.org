Executive Summary

Customs clearance in New Jersey is the federal United States Customs function through which goods entering or leaving the country through New Jersey seaports, airports and related Customs facilities are presented, entered, reviewed and released or exported under U.S. Customs law. It is not a separate New Jersey state Customs system. The controlling Customs authority is U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP), while New Jersey can remain relevant to sales and use tax, commercial operations, warehousing, logistics and the destination use of imported goods.

Operationally, Customs clearance commonly begins before goods arrive at a New Jersey gateway. Businesses need to identify the goods, route, commercial parties, importer of record, customs broker or other filing party, applicable Harmonized Tariff Schedule classification, Customs value, country of origin, admissibility requirements, relevant Partner Government Agency controls and supporting records required for U.S. entry or export processing.

New Jersey's Customs significance is shaped by the Port of New York/Newark, a combined federal CBP operational environment spanning New York and New Jersey under Port Code 4601. New Jersey contains Port Newark and Elizabeth marine terminals, regional container freight stations, bonded warehouses and foreign-trade-zone facilities within the New York/Newark port complex. Newark Liberty International Airport is also a significant New Jersey international air-cargo gateway. CBP has moved air-cargo entrance and clearance functions from the former Newark Airport Centralized Air Cargo Examination Station to the CBP SHIPS Office at Newark Liberty International Airport Terminal B.

New Jersey also has a separate state tax interface. The New Jersey Division of Taxation states that use tax is owed by residents and businesses that buy taxable products or services outside New Jersey, online or by mail, and bring them into the state, where no sales tax or tax at a lower rate was paid. The current New Jersey sales and use-tax rate is 6.625 percent. This creates a two-layer operating context: federal Customs clearance at the U.S. border and separate New Jersey sales or use-tax analysis where qualifying property or services are used, stored or consumed in New Jersey.

Purpose

The purpose of Customs clearance through New Jersey is to ensure that goods entering or leaving the United States via New Jersey seaports, airports and associated Customs facilities are processed through the federal U.S. Customs framework in a controlled, documented and legally coherent manner.

It exists to support admissible and properly entered goods, correct tariff and duty treatment, lawful cargo release or export, effective Customs control and alignment between the importer or exporter, the shipment, CBP, carriers, customs brokers and any relevant regulatory authorities. Where goods are brought into New Jersey for storage, use or consumption, the process can also require separate New Jersey sales or use-tax consideration.

ChecklistWhat are the goods? Which New Jersey marine terminal, airport, bonded facility, foreign-trade zone or logistics location is involved? Who is acting as importer of record, exporter, customs broker, carrier, consignee or logistics representative? Are commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading or air waybill, entry data, tariff classification, Customs value, country of origin and product-admissibility records consistent? Are any Partner Government Agency licences, certificates or controls required? Has the CBP entry, security filing and local gateway release process been planned before cargo arrives? Will goods or services be used in New Jersey, and has New Jersey sales or use-tax treatment been assessed separately? Is there a clear release and record-retention workflow?
