Executive Summary

Customs clearance in Pennsylvania is the federal United States Customs function through which goods entering or leaving the country through Pennsylvania seaports, airports and related Customs facilities are presented, entered, reviewed and released or exported under U.S. Customs law. It is not a separate Pennsylvania state Customs system. The controlling Customs authority is U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP), while Pennsylvania can remain relevant to sales and use tax, commercial operations, warehousing, distribution and the destination use of imported goods.

Operationally, Customs clearance commonly begins before goods arrive at a Pennsylvania gateway. Businesses need to identify the goods, route, commercial parties, importer of record, customs broker or other filing party, applicable Harmonized Tariff Schedule classification, Customs value, country of origin, admissibility requirements, relevant Partner Government Agency controls and supporting records required for U.S. entry or export processing.

Pennsylvania's Customs significance is shaped by the Philadelphia CBP port environment and its connected regional logistics infrastructure. Philadelphia, CBP Port Code 1101, handles maritime and air-cargo Customs activity, including commercial cargo, cargo-release and Customs examination functions. The official CBP port record identifies separate maritime and air-cargo contact channels and a Centralized Examination Station at East Coast Warehouse for seaport cargo. Pennsylvania also has Pittsburgh International Airport and inland distribution, manufacturing and rail/logistics connections that support international trade movements across the Commonwealth.

Pennsylvania also has a separate state tax interface. The Pennsylvania Department of Revenue states that use tax is due when Pennsylvania sales tax is not charged on a taxable item or service delivered into or used in Pennsylvania. This includes taxable property purchased from sellers outside the United States and delivered into or used in Pennsylvania. The base rate is 6 percent; a further 1 percent local tax can apply in Allegheny County and a further 2 percent local tax can apply in Philadelphia. This creates a two-layer operating context: federal Customs clearance at the U.S. border and separate Pennsylvania sales or use-tax analysis where goods are used, stored or consumed in Pennsylvania.

Purpose

The purpose of Customs clearance through Pennsylvania is to ensure that goods entering or leaving the United States via Pennsylvania seaports, airports and associated Customs facilities are processed through the federal U.S. Customs framework in a controlled, documented and legally coherent manner.

It exists to support admissible and properly entered goods, correct tariff and duty treatment, lawful cargo release or export, effective Customs control and alignment between the importer or exporter, shipment, CBP, carriers, customs brokers and relevant regulatory authorities. Where goods are brought into Pennsylvania for storage, use or consumption, the process can also require separate Pennsylvania sales or use-tax consideration.

Primary Outcome

A goods movement lawfully entered, released, exported or otherwise processed under federal U.S. Customs requirements through a Pennsylvania port, airport or related Customs facility, supported by correct entry data, documentary evidence, tariff and Customs-value treatment, admissibility readiness and, where applicable, separate Pennsylvania sales or use-tax compliance consideration.
