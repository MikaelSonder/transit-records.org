Executive Summary

Customs clearance in Washington State is the federal United States Customs function through which goods entering or leaving the country through Washington seaports, airports, Canada-border facilities and related Customs locations are presented, entered, reviewed and released or exported under U.S. Customs law. It is not a separate Washington state Customs system. The controlling Customs authority is U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP), while Washington State can remain relevant to sales and use tax, commercial operations, Pacific trade, Canada-border logistics and the destination use of imported goods.

Operationally, Customs clearance commonly begins before goods arrive at a Washington gateway. Businesses need to identify the goods, route, commercial parties, importer of record, customs broker or other filing party, applicable Harmonized Tariff Schedule classification, Customs value, country of origin, admissibility requirements, relevant Partner Government Agency controls and supporting records required for U.S. entry or export processing.

Washington's Customs significance is shaped by its Pacific Northwest maritime, air-cargo and Canada-border environment. Seattle is CBP Port Code 3001 and Tacoma is CBP Port Code 3002. CBP identifies both as service-port environments, meaning they provide a full range of cargo processing functions including inspections, entry, collections and verification. The Seattle and Tacoma operational area includes seaport cargo, air cargo, cargo security, trade functions and Centralized Examination Stations. Washington also has northern land ports and cross-border freight links with Canada, together with major rail, road, warehouse and distribution connections.

Washington also has a separate state tax interface. Washington Department of Revenue states that use tax applies to the use of goods or certain services in Washington when sales tax has not been paid. Use tax can arise from purchases in another state, from out-of-state Internet or mail-order sellers, or from sellers not authorised to collect sales tax. The state component of Washington use tax is 6.5 percent, with local rates affecting the combined rate based on where the goods are first used. A credit can generally be available for qualifying sales or use tax paid to another state or country. This creates a two-layer operating context: federal Customs clearance at the U.S. border and separate Washington use-tax analysis where goods are used, stored or consumed in Washington.

Purpose

The purpose of Customs clearance through Washington is to ensure that goods entering or leaving the United States via Washington seaports, airports, Canada-border facilities and associated Customs locations are processed through the federal U.S. Customs framework in a controlled, documented and legally coherent manner.

It exists to support admissible and properly entered goods, correct tariff and duty treatment, lawful cargo release or export, effective Customs control and alignment between the importer or exporter, shipment, CBP, carriers, customs brokers and relevant regulatory authorities. Where goods are brought into Washington for storage, use or consumption, the process can also require separate Washington use-tax consideration.

Primary Outcome

A goods movement lawfully entered, released, exported or otherwise processed under federal U.S. Customs requirements through a Washington port, airport, Canada-border crossing or related Customs facility, supported by correct entry data, documentary evidence, tariff and Customs-value treatment, admissibility readiness and, where applicable, separate Washington use-tax compliance consideration.
